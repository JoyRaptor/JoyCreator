# Avatar Studio: the four stages (2026-11-21)

The owner's brief, in his words: "The first thing a user will want to do is load something all in, followed by wiring it
all up, then tweaking to make sure it looks good in one place, then looks good in all poses, then moves naturally and
behaves well. Then play with it and puppeteering becomes priority." And: "Consolidate. Think about flow."

This is the structure built from that, in the web pilot (`tools/avatarlab/al-stages.js`). It is not owner-approved yet;
the earlier layout stays one tap away (Menu, "One long card per part") so he can compare.

## The idea

Every control answers one of three questions. It lives in the stage that asks that question and nowhere else.

| Stage | The question | The picture | A drag on the picture |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Set up** | What exists, and where does it hinge? | still, shows **pins** | moves a pin |
| **Fit** | Does it look right: here, then in every pose? | still, shows **the box** on the part in hand | moves the part; corners resize, the handle turns |
| **Motion** | How does it move? | **alive** | pulls the character, as in Perform |
| **Perform** | (play it, record it) | alive | puppeteers |

His six steps map onto them: bring in and wire up = Set up; right in one place, then in every pose = Fit;
moves and behaves = Motion; play = Perform.

Three things say where you are, and all three are always on screen:

- **What**: the small body map (kept as it was: it is also the way out of anything), a tap on the picture, the Layers button.
- **Which job**: the four-way row at the top of the panel, in reach of the thumb. It replaces Build / Perform in the top bar.
- **Which pose** (Fit only): a row on the picture: Standing, each stance, and + for a new one. In a stance, what is
  moved is moved for that stance.

So the old floating modes are gone, because each is now a place: "Try it" is Motion, "Move it" is Fit, "work on a
stance" is a pose chosen in Fit, "Pick a piece" is a tap on the picture. Nothing floats over the feet in Set up.

## Rules the cards follow

1. A section belongs to one stage: `ui.lab('Pins', 'setup')`. A few small blocks that are honestly both (how the mouth
   talks, eyelids, a limb's drawings, the head's angle pad) show in two. Navigation (the face parts of the head, the
   limbs of the body) and the whole Ground card show in all.
2. Sections are open. A long one can fold; the seldom-used ones share one drawer, "More"; a card with three or more
   sections gets shortcuts under the four-way row.
3. In Fit the box is simply there on whatever is in hand. A limb outside a stance slides whole (pins and all) instead.
   A card that never offered fitting (eyes, brows, jaw, mouth, tail, the whole head) gets "Fit it by hand" and the stack.
4. In Motion every card ends with "Play with it" (the turn pad, Hold to talk, the buttons, moods), and the "Try:"
   sliders work on the living picture.
5. A part with nothing in a stage says so in one line and offers the stages where it has something.
6. Walking is one card (the Ground bar on the map), the same in every stage.

## Open questions for the owner

- The names: Set up / Fit / Motion / Perform.
- The four-way row sits at the top of the panel (thumb reach). A bar along the very bottom of the screen is the other
  place it could go.
- Motion being alive means pins cannot be dragged there; they are one tap away in Set up.
- A stance's own numbers (how far it tips, how long it takes) are all in Fit · Body, in one block.
