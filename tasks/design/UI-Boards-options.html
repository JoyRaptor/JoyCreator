Satellite pill, crop marks, frame wheel, pen-down fade

  • Controls: a floating pill 10 dp off the board's left edge holding the kind icon, then the frame wheel (animation) or the feature switch (image, tile, sprite), then lock, then export. It does not hop: while the pen is down on the board everything fades to 12% and the film strip folds to its ruler.
  • At rest: an animation board is only its film icon (plus a small frame number when it is not on frame 1). Image, tile and sprite boards show crop marks and their icon. Soft brackets fade in around a resting animation board when a pen comes within 48 dp.
  • Hover to play: hover the resting film icon with a pen or mouse and the board loops in place, for talking over a screen recording.
  • Tiling on: the tile fills the whole canvas edge to edge. Only the switch (on) and the selection ring around the original tile remain.
  • One export icon, app-wide: the Studio's film-strip export mark.
  • The rest: title in halo type, size as a corner readout, pegs fused to an outline film strip, runner / mountain marks in the layer column, the grid shelf under sprite boards, the Studio's export sheet.

      My recommendation, before you picked
      
A, Bookmark, with the frame wheel and the pen-down fade

      
    
        
  • It is your own layout. You described an icon at the top-left with the frame number, lock and export stacked under it. A is that, and nothing ever moves, so your thumb learns where export is after a day.
    • 
        
  • The frame wheel (from D) makes the number do work. Drag it up or down to flip frames without reaching for the strip. That is how you will check a pose one-handed.
    • 
        
  • The pen-down fade (from B) is the quietest rule that keeps things put. Pen down anywhere on the board and everything sinks to a whisper and the strip folds to a ruler; lift, and it is all back where it was. Fading only near the tab still leaves a full film strip glowing under a board you are inking.
    • 
        
  • What I would avoid: the spotlight dim changes how your colours look next to the board, and the hop moves controls at the moment you reach for them.
