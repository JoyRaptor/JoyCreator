One brush editor that shows what a brush does

Every brush, every engine, the same editor. Seeing replaces reading: one gorgeous test stroke that crosses a set of colours, redrawn while a slider moves; the tip seen from the side; slider ends drawn as tiny strokes. Everything here is live: drag sliders, draw on the test area with a mouse or pen, and tap an option to pick it. Your picks build the line at the bottom; copy it and send it to me.

AThe test stroke

One shape for every brush, made to show the most in one pass: it starts feather-light and presses harder, the pen tilts along it, it crosses itself (so you see how paint builds on paint), and it ends with a sudden lift, not a fade. Pick the shape.

BThe colours it crosses

Painted bands under the stroke show pickup and smudging: a wet brush drags colour out of them, a glaze darkens through them, a dry pencil skips over them. Each set is built on a colour-theory rule, so they sit together like a designer's palette, never like a flag.

CTry it: a brush you can tune

A stand-in brush (not the real engine yet) so you can feel the idea: move any slider and the stroke redraws while you drag. Draw on the test area to try your own marks. The cross-section beside it is the tip seen edge-on; the footprint shows the tip lying down as the pen tilts.

DSlider ends

Every slider shows what its two ends look like, so you don't have to guess what "Spacing" or "Flow" means. Pick how the ends are drawn.

EThe editor, at true size

Three layouts for the one editor, drawn at the Note 9's real size (411 × 846). All three keep the drawer see-through, put the pen pad, the tip's cross-section and the test stroke together at the top, and use the same controls for every brush; a setting a brush doesn't have is simply left out.
