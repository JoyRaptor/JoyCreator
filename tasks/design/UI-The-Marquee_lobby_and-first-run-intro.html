02

The bottom third

You were right — it was empty and it read as unfinished, because I stopped where your brainstorm stopped. Here's what belongs there and why, in the order it appears.

i
Carry on — the row you already asked for

Recents, mixed, corner-coded. Cards are 100px and the corner cut stayed here — it does its best work when four different kinds of thing sit side by side.

ii
New — because a lobby has two jobs, and I only built one

Carrying on and starting fresh are different intents, and the old screen had no answer for the second one except going into a room first. Four nouns: Recording · Project · Character · Import.

They are grey buttons with full-strength coloured glyphs, not coloured buttons. That keeps your no-faded-colour rule and keeps the screen's one saturated control — the hero's action — unmistakably primary.

iii
Joybot's one line — the app noticing something

A single sentence about your actual project, with one action and a dismiss. It uses a tool the app already has, it advertises the AI without a tour, and it's the only thing on the screen that changes because of what you did rather than what you tapped.

It rotates through real observations — missing captions, unused recordings, a sheet with unnamed cells, an export that failed. It never invents a number.

iv
The floor — unchanged, and that is the point

Five words that never move. Now with drawn glyphs at 13px against 11.5px type, so the icon supports the word instead of competing with it.

v
What the hairline bought

Moving the export from a 58px frosted strip to a 2px bar under the status bar freed the height that the New row and Joybot's line now occupy. Your instinct about the thin bar wasn't just aesthetic — it paid for the bottom third.

?
Why the hero goes full bleed — and the one cost

No reason not to, and three reasons to. It gives the picture ~11% more area on a 390pt screen; it stops the lobby reading as a stack of cards, which is the single most common AI-design tell; and a live viewfinder inset in a rounded box looks like a photo of a camera rather than a camera.

The cost: the phone's rounded corners clip the image, so nothing important can live in the top 14px or the corners. That's why the LIVE tag is inset and the category signal became a bottom bar. It is also the one place on this screen that breaks the 17px gutter — deliberately, once.

04

The motion system

Numbers, not adjectives — so an outside agent implementing this has nothing to invent. Sourced from emilkowalski/skills, reconciled against what Joy Creator actually does.

WhereDurationCurveWhy
The four curves. Nothing uses a raw CSS keyword.
Entering, leaving—cubic-bezier(.23,1,.32,1)Starts fast, lands soft. The default for anything appearing.
Moving on screen—cubic-bezier(.77,0,.175,1)Eases both ends, because the thing existed before and after.
Drawers, sheets—cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1)The drawer curve. Long tail, so a sheet settles rather than stops.
Never—ease-inIt delays motion at exactly the moment the eye is watching. Not used anywhere in this app.
Durations. Hard ceiling: 300ms for anything you do more than once a session.
Any press140msease-outscale(.97). Every tappable thing, no exceptions.
Tool row swaps170msease-outCross-fade only, no movement. Your open question from record 03 — this is my answer.
Popovers, pickers220msease-outFrom scale(.95), never from scale(0).
Marquee title swap260msease-outWeight, size and colour interpolate together on one curve.
Hero cross-fade260msease-outWith filter: blur(2px) through the middle — see below.
Drawers, the timeline sheet320msdrawerThe one thing allowed past 300ms, because it travels a long way.
First-run slideshow560msease-outSeen once. Nobody is waiting on it. Delight is allowed here and nowhere else.
Rules that matter more than the numbers
Cross-fade blur——Two states fading over each other always read as two states. A 2px blur through the middle makes it read as one thing changing. Costs nothing, fixes the thing no amount of easing fixes.
Only transform and opacity——Anything else recalculates layout. On a phone that is also encoding video, that is dropped frames in the preview.
Never transition: all——It animates properties you did not mean to animate, including ones that trigger layout.
Interruptible——CSS transitions retarget mid-flight; keyframes restart from zero. Anything you can tap twice quickly uses a transition.
Stagger40msease-outBetween recents cards on first paint. Decorative only — never block a tap waiting for a stagger to finish.
Things done 100× a day0ms—Undo. Play. Split. Tapping a cell to key it. No animation, ever. Motion on a hundred-times-a-day action is just latency wearing a costume.
Reduced motion——Fewer and gentler, not zero. Opacity and colour survive; travel and scale are dropped.

The one that changes the editor most: things done a hundred times a day get no animation at all. Split, undo, play and keying a sprite cell at the playhead are in that bracket. A 200ms flourish on the tap you make two hundred times an afternoon does not read as polish — it reads as a slow app.

What the audit found in my own work

Run against tastemaker's 51 anti-slop gates, MengTo's slop audit and jakubkrehel's surface rules. Findings against my three previous records, highest impact first. The rule those audits share: default to removing, not replacing.

FindingWhereWhat I did
Emoji used as icons72 across three records. 🎬 🎥 📚 for rooms, on phones and in tables. Replaced with a drawn 24-icon inline sprite — 1.6px stroke, currentColor, one <symbol> set. Matches Sprite Lab's own spec, which already said to do this.fixed
Decorative stackingMastheads carried a radial glow and a second glow and scanlines. Three effects, one job. One light source: a single vertical gradient. The noise and scanlines are gone from page chrome and survive only in the grounds catalog, where they're the subject.fixed
Card repetitionEvery explanation was a rounded box in a grid, whatever it contained. Prose lists with a marker column where the content is argument. A card should mean "this is a thing", not "this is text".fixed
Identical section eyebrowsEvery section opened with the same mono-uppercase pill in the accent colour. Dropped the pill. A number and a heading. The accent now appears where it carries information.fixed
Accent footprint over budgetGate 19 caps accent at ~5%. The first lobby had four gradient buttons in the New row plus a gradient hero action plus gradient section chrome. New row became grey buttons with coloured glyphs. One saturated control per screen, and it's the primary action.fixed
No motion spec at allThe mockups animated on a curve I made up. Section 04. Four curves, eight durations, six rules, all sourced.fixed
Borders where shadows belongEvery card had a 1px border on all sides, including over imagery. Cards on dark ground use a single white ring at 8% via outline, offset −1px — it survives over a thumbnail where a solid border shows as a seam. Borders now only divide.fixed
Inline hex instead of tokensGate 48. Dozens of literal colours in style attributes. Partly. The room gradients are tokens; per-card thumbnails still carry literals because they stand in for artwork. In the Android build every one becomes a token.partly
Indigo-to-purple gradientGate 14 rejects it by name as the AI gradient. Joybot is violet→indigo. Kept — you picked it. The gate is about hero gradients on landing pages; here it's a room code carrying information, and it's the one room whose identity is "thinking". Flagging it so it's a decision, not an oversight.kept
Two-line clickable textGate 26. Some floor and tool labels wrapped at 390px. All tap targets are single-line now, with ellipsis before wrap.fixed
Concentric radiusNested corners were arbitrary — a 12px card inside a 14px panel. Applied outer = inner + gap. The recents card is 12px inside a 17px gutter; the hero has no radius because it has no inset.fixed
Invented metricsGate 47 forbids fabricated proof. Checked. Every number in all four records is counted from the repo or is clearly a mockup's sample data. Joybot's suggestion line is the risk here — it must only ever say things it verified.held

The audit I did not take

One skill mandates a mid-page "tagline reveal" with word-by-word opacity on scroll, and another bans background gradients outright. Both are landing-page conversion rules — they exist to sell a product to a stranger. These pages are a working design record for one person who already owns the thing. Importing them would have made the work worse, not more original.

Which is the actual lesson from reading seven of them: a rule is only as good as the situation it was written for. The motion numbers travel. The conversion structure doesn't.
