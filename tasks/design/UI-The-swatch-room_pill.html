The theme system

33theme attributes, 9 themes

One attribute, colorHeading, is used 314 times in the layouts. That single line is why every settings row, every section header and every card title in the legacy app is red. Change it and the whole app changes — but it only reaches the screens that were built this way.

Reaches: Home, Records, Remote, Settings, Smart Detection, onboarding.

SpriteLab's system

16tokens, one file, written rules

8 neutrals, 6 section accents, 2 state colours — and, crucially, a rule that separates them: cyan always means "this is selected", pink always means "this is what's playing". A room's colour may never be a colour that already means a state.

Reaches: Sprite Lab and the sprite drawer. Nothing else. Yet.

02

The palette you already chose

Fourteen colours, pulled from your Bible Study project and written into the brand brief in July 2026. Any two adjacent ones gradient cleanly — that was the whole point of ordering them this way. Tap one to make it the accent for the rest of this page. Everything below repaints.

The four state colours are not on this wheel, and shouldn't be. Selected, playing, warning and destructive need to mean the same thing in every room. If "playing" is pink in the Studio and pink also means "you are in the Recorder", the user has to learn two pinks. Proposed and used by SpriteLab today: cyan = selected, pink = live, amber = careful, red = destroys something.

04

Grounds & surfaces

What a panel is made of, before any colour lands on it. This is the decision that sets the whole feeling — and the one nobody has made. Right now the app uses flat, lifted and frosted in different rooms. Tap the ones you want.

05

Four control languages

The same fourteen controls, built four ways. Identical parts, identical order, so the only variable is the language. These are not themes — a theme changes colour, a language changes shape, weight, border, case and how a thing announces that it is on. Pick a language and pick an accent and you have decided most of the app.

06

Room colours

Your brief asked for this in July and it was never decided, which is why nothing could be mass-applied. Each room gets a two-colour gradient so you always know where you are. This is a proposal, not a ruling — the greens are locked to the Studio because the editor already owns them and re-teaching that is expensive. Tap any room you disagree with and I'll know to re-pitch it.

Why the Studio keeps green. It is the one colour association in this app that has been trained into you over months of daily use — play is green, the export arrow is green, the timeline's selected clip is green. Moving it would cost more than it buys. Everything else is free to change.
