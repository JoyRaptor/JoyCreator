## One snapping authority: written up for an Opus session (2026-11-24, Sonnet lane, queue row 25)
Not a contained change, so not started. Facts a builder needs, from the code today:
- **Where snapping lives now:** `FaditorEditorActivity` (540 mentions), `timeline/EditorTimelineView` (50), `overlay/TextOverlayLayer` (30), `model/Clip` (24), `overlay/PreviewHandlesOverlay` (18, a private `snapRotation`), `crop/CropOverlayView` (16), `puppet/PuppetDrawerTabs` (3), word sync and onset code (`transcript/WordSyncMode`, `WordSyncOnsets`, `waveform/OnsetDetector.snapToleranceMs`), and a private pivot snap in `TextOverlayItem.snapPivotAxis`. The magnet panel (`SnapSettingsSheet`, `tools/SnapSettings`, `Kind.BEATS` and the rest) already holds the person's switches and strengths.
- **What "one authority" means here:** every drag that can snap (playhead, clip edges, objects on the canvas, rotation, crop, tape ends) asks ONE pure class for "given this position, these candidate points, this tolerance and these switches, where do I land and what did I snap to", and the magnet indicator reads what it answered. Today each caller keeps its own candidates and tolerance, which is why the same drag can snap in one place and not another.
- **Why not now:** it touches the editor core and the timeline's touch code that the timeline-clock work (`TimeMap`, STU-5's other row) also touches; the feel (tolerance in pixels, strength) can only be judged by hand on a phone. Do it after the clock work, one editor session at a time, with a pure `SnapAuthority` class and tests that pin today's tolerances first.

## One snapping authority: BUILT 2026-11-24 (Opus, queue row 26)
`studiokit/.../tools/SnapAuthority.java` is now the one place that decides which candidate a snap lands on, for the timeline, the canvas and the handles. Each surface still chooses its own candidates and reach (its feel and its switch in `SnapSettings`); the authority picks the winner.
- **Routed through it:** beats (`BeatDetector.snap`), word onsets (`OnsetDetector.snap`) and the word-sync magnet (it used its own copy of the search; now it asks the same question, so it lights exactly when the snap happens), the timeline's butt, align and fade-knob magnets (`LayerGestureController`), canvas boxes against the frame and other objects (`TransformOverlayView.snapAxis` / `snapAxisTo`), rotation steps (`TransformOverlayView.snapRotation`) and cardinals (`PreviewHandlesOverlay.snapRotation`), text-overlay and sprite centres and drop times, opacity keyframes (`Clip.opacityKeyframeIndexAt`).
- **Nothing a person relies on moved:** `tools/jvm-harness/SnapAuthorityTest.java` (`run-snap.sh`, in the cloud Studio run) holds a frozen copy of each surface's old code and compares it with the authority on 40,000 random inputs each, including exact ties and the exact edge of the reach, plus hand-worked pins and a check that every surface still routes through it (39 checks). The old beat, onset, gesture-angle and scale-snap suites still pass.
- **The one deliberate change:** a TEXT overlay's drop-time snap now takes the nearest edge within 250 ms. It used to keep the last edge within reach (its helper updated a copy of the best distance), while the sprite layer's identical snap always took the nearest. Not made selectable: it is a bug, and the two layers now agree.
- **Not routed yet (each keeps its own arithmetic, listed so the next step knows):** the timeline's home-snap and trim-home comparisons (`LayerGestureController`, a single target each), PiP and puppet key-time snaps in `FaditorEditorActivity` (`snapPipKeyTime` takes the FIRST key within 66 ms, `puppetKeyTimeNow` the nearest: moving them would change which key a press lands on, so that wants the owner's feel-check first, with the old rule kept selectable), crop's centre snap (raw 36 px, its own toggle), the scale-detent and gradient-stop and split-height detents, and the 90-degree cardinal detent with hysteresis (`TransformQuad`).
- **Feel changes are for later and must stay selectable:** making every canvas surface use the panel's Gentle / Normal / Strong (today the text and sprite centres ignore it) would change how a drag feels; do it behind the panel with the old reach kept as an option.
- **Owed (phone, hover lane):** a feel-check on the Note 9 that snapping feels the same as before on the timeline (move a clip against a neighbour and across lanes, drag a fade knob, beat snap on), on the canvas (drag a picture to the centre and against another object, rotate past 45 and level), and a text overlay dropped near two edges lands on the nearer one.

## One time authority, the PREVIEW half: built 2026-11-24 (Opus, queue row 27)
`model/PreviewClock.java` (pure) answers the live preview's questions with the export's own numbers (`TimeMap`): a player position to the editor clock and back, the seam's transition, and the blend's length and start in the clip's media time. The export was not touched.
- **Routed (FaditorEditorActivity):** `getAbsolutePlayheadMs` and `segmentRelativeForAbsolute` (the playhead clock, overlays and captions timing, seeks), the playhead during a blend (`getOutputPositionForCurrentPlayback`), `getTransitionAtSeam`, the playback poll's blend window and the scrub preview's.
- **Nothing moved where the preview was already right:** `tools/jvm-harness/PreviewClockTest.java` (`run-previewclock.sh`, in the cloud Studio run) compares frozen copies of the old preview code with PreviewClock on 4,000 random timelines at normal speed with no loops and one in-budget transition: identical.
- **What now changes, each to the export's answer (pinned by hand-worked cases):**
  1. A clip that is not at normal speed: the blend opened at the wrong moment (at 2x about a second early, at 0.5x not at all). It now opens where the export overlaps the clips.
  2. A transition longer than the clip after it blends for the export's clamped length (500 ms into a 500 ms clip, not 800).
  3. Two transitions on one seam: the preview plays the one the export plays (the first; it played the last).
  4. After a clip with a loop extension, the playhead, captions and overlays used the clip start without the loop, so they ran early by the loop's length; they now use the timeline's own start.
  5. The playhead during a blend shows the editor clock; it used to drop back by the earlier transitions (and by double the speed division) and jump forward at the blend's end.
- **Still open (each named in the 11-24 scout map; display or edge cases):** the seam owner when dragging the playhead by pixel (the earlier clip; a tap gives the later), rounding (cast vs round, D9), the gapless engine clamping a seek with a timeline length in media units (D10: a tap in the second half of a 2x clip on a gapless project lands at the middle), the export clamping a clip to its real source length while the preview does not (D11), B's start offset after a live blend, cuts not shortening the timeline span, E5 (no volume curve or fades during the blend, preview and export alike), D2 (owner ruling), D3.
- **Owed on the Note 9 (phone lane):** a project with a clip at 2x, a 600 ms transition, then a normal clip: play through the seam and confirm the blend starts about 0.6 s before the cut (not a second early), the playhead does not jump at the blend's end, and an export of the same project shows the blend at the same moment. Then a 0.5x clip before a transition (the blend must happen), and a looped clip followed by a clip with a caption (the caption must appear on its word, not early).

## Export speed, checked 2026-11-24 (Opus, queue row 28): fine for launch; the next lead needs the phone
- **Can it be measured off the phone?** No. The export is Media3 Transformer driving the phone's own video encoder and GPU; the cloud runners and this PC have neither, so a host or cloud benchmark would measure the wrong machine.
- **What is already measured:** after the per-frame picture copies came out of four passes (E17, 11-12), a 9 min 20 s export ran on the Note 9 at an even 1.2x real time with no slowdown over its length; the profile put Canvas blits and GL draws on top and copies at about 1 percent. A phone that exports a minute of film in under a minute meets the owner's "one tap, fast" for launch.
- **The next lead, for the phone lane:** the one-pass GPU stage (draw the overlays in the same GL pass instead of a Canvas blit per layer). Measure first on the Note 9: export the same 1 min project with 0, 3 and 6 text and picture layers, note seconds and frame time (`dumpsys gfxinfo` is not enough: use the export's own per-part timing in its log) and the battery temperature; if the time grows with layers, the Canvas blits are the cost and the one-pass stage is worth building; if not, leave it.

## The held frame at a cut (phone lane, 11-24): older than PreviewClock, a design call
At the end of a non-live blend the preview holds clip B at the blend's length (B's head was drawn during the blend) and then starts B from its beginning, so the first frame after the cut shows B about one blend-length ahead and B's first second plays twice. Not caused by the PreviewClock commits (no line of the hand-off changed; the blend window is pinned unchanged at normal speed). The fix is the structural one already listed: the editor's timeline does not overlap the clips at a transition while the export does. Choosing between "the timeline shortens at a transition, as the file does" and "the blend shows only A's tail, B starts clean" is the owner's call; see the STU-1 note of 11-24 for the evidence.

## Paths, step 3 (2026-11-24, Opus, queue row 30): one drawing for preview and export, hidden
- `model/path/PathPlan` (pure) works out, once per frame size, everything that decides a path's look: the outline in pixels (`StudioPath.polyline`, 48 points a curve), the line width as a share of the frame HEIGHT (so a 720p preview and a 4K export draw the same thing), the colours, and the rule that only a closed path is filled. `overlay/PathDraw` is the one routine that turns that plan into Canvas calls (fill, then a round-ended line), for both surfaces.
- Parity test: `run-pathplan.sh` 12 (the export's outline is the preview's scaled point for point; line width follows height; same colours; open paths never filled; empty paths draw nothing; the draw routine reads only the plan; the switch is off).
- **Not wired into the preview or the export yet, on purpose:** where a path sits among the layers (its z order, and so which preview view and which export pass draws it) is the owner's pick E on the options page (its own Shapes lane, or with the pictures). Wiring is one call in the chosen preview layer and one in `CompositeExportOverlay` (plus its "nothing to draw" check), both through `PathDraw.draw`.

## Owner ruling 11-27: transitions overlap (choice A), with a ripple check
Clips overlap at a transition, so the project gets shorter by the transition, the way the export already works. That is
safe only with a check. When adding, lengthening or removing a transition would change the project's length and there
are layers above it (images, sprites, text, audio) placed after that point, show a light, unintrusive check. It says
what will happen ("this makes the project 1 s shorter; 6 layers start after this point") and offers, right there:
move those layers with their clips, or leave them where they are. A "don't ask again" switch for people who know what
they're doing, and the whole change is one undo step. Owner's options page first (wording, placement, default), then
the build. This also fixes the odd frame at the cut (preview shows the second clip's head twice).
