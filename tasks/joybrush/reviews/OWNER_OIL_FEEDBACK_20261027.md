## Carries: his model (design input, his forensics)
- He could not tell what the slider did on its own, but liked what happened: painting yellow, a light pass over a green line
  carried green only in the middle, where the brush pushes most. Pressing harder after passing the green, the sides stayed
  yellow because they had not crossed the green.
- Brush shape decides where carrying happens. A brush with an even (flat) end carries most from its middle. A brush whose
  hairs all end evenly carries according to tilt: the leaning side has the most force and does most of the carrying, and the
  other side stays lighter.
- Unless the curve of the stroke pushes paint outward, which smears it into a thicker edge on the outside.
- "Direction and tilt force play a pretty sophisticated cross-interaction."
- Next for the oil lane: make Carries readable on its own (the slider's effect was not discernible), driven by per-hair force
  from tilt, brush end shape and stroke curvature, with build-up on the outside of curves.
