# Layer operations: merge down, flatten, lock, apply mask, whole-drawing export (lead spec, 2026-10-18)

Owner priority: "all the layers and boards working flawlessly together." None of these exist in code today (checked
2026-10-18). This spec records the owner's decisions of 2026-10-18 and the engine design. Builder: lead (hot files).

## 0. Owner decisions (2026-10-18, condensed from his answers)
1. **Merge Down with editable lines: the lower layer stays editable.** He asked how one layer can hold "raster vector raster
   vector" without being two layers. Answer given: under R51 a layer already IS an ordered stack of thin sheets (its base
   pixels, then each line or pixel slab in drawing order: NATIVE_LINES v1.2). Merging puts the upper layer's sheets on top
   of the lower layer's, in order, so nothing slips underneath and the picture does not change.
2. **Merge Down with realistic paint: lead's judgment**, keeping as much live physics (impasto, crushed tooth, water) as the
   Note 9 can afford. Decision below (§2.3).
3. **Flatten and hidden layers: ask like Photoshop the first time, with "Remember my choice"**; changeable later in
   Settings. Photoshop users get what they know; nobody is locked in.
4. **Whole-drawing export: a choice at export time** (one flattened picture, or a layered file), remembering the last one
   used; one tap to swap.

## 1. The model (why merge keeps lines)
A plane (layer × cel) = base ∘ items in seq order (NATIVE_LINES_V12). A layer's group settings apply to the composited
layer as a whole: blend mode, opacity, mask, clipping.

## 2. Merge Down (upper U onto lower L), ONE undo step, one sentence when something became pixels
1. **U is plain** (normal blend, opacity 1, no mask, not clipped, not hidden): for every plane pair, U's base becomes a slab
   on L at L's next seq, then U's items follow with fresh seqs above it in their own order (records keep their frozen
   brushes; slabs keep operators). Everything stays editable. Tiles: L's tile = U over L (the GPU's normal composite).
2. **U has group settings**: U's planes are rendered as one picture each (its blend, opacity, mask and clip applied) and
   added to L as ONE slab per plane at L's next seq, with U's blend as the slab operator when it is one of the five
   (normal, erase, behind, multiply, screen). A blend mode outside those five makes L's items flatten too (its tiles are
   simply composited). Sentence: "Lines on <U> became paint so its <setting> could be kept."
3. **Realistic paint (lead's call):** L's media payload stays live (re-wettable, impasto, crushed tooth) on every tile U
   does not paint. On tiles where U adds paint over L's payload, the payload is settled to dry pixels (water settled as a
   save does) and those tiles become ordinary pixels, said once: "Where <U> covered realistic paint, that paint is now
   dry." U's own payload: carried to L where L has none on that tile (a plain U only), else settled. Rationale: two
   payloads in one tile would need a pigment-stacking physics the Note 9 can't afford per frame; the look never changes.
4. **Animation (owner, 2026-10-18):** "merging down with animation layers would preserve the animation, but all the merging
   would happen just on the frame that's currently active." So while an animation frame is the one being worked on, Merge
   Down merges ONLY that frame: the upper layer's current-frame cel goes into the lower layer's current-frame cel (same
   sheets rule as §2.1–2.3), the upper keeps its other frames (and stays a layer), every other frame is untouched. One undo.
   If the upper layer has no frame there (static in that board), its pixels inside the board rect merge into the lower's
   current frame. The whole-layer merge below applies only when no animation frame is active.
   **Boards (whole-layer merge):** pairs are matched per document cel: shared ↔ shared, and for each board both animate in, frame ↔ frame by
   frame id. If U animates in a board L does not, L first gains frames for that board (each a copy of L's pixels and items
   in the board rect), in the same step. Held regions follow the owner's board model (JB-3.00a).
5. Refused in words: a locked L or U; the bottom layer; a pen down.

## 3. Flatten = FREEZE (owner, 2026-10-18, second message)
Why people flatten (his words, condensed): to save space; to **freeze the look** before something complex on top, so the
next operation behaves as expected (WYSIWYG, especially with transparency: "if it looks good on a checkerboard, freeze
that", no white fringing or artefacts); and to keep things simple, which on a phone also means RAM.
So Flatten does NOT keep sheets: all VISIBLE layers become ONE layer of plain pixels. Editable lines, slabs and realistic
paint state are all baked (water settled as a save does), which frees their memory. ONE undo step brings everything back.
Hidden layers: the first time, a sheet asks "Keep hidden layers / Discard hidden layers" with a "Remember my choice" box;
Settings → Layers holds the remembered choice ("Ask", "Keep", "Discard"). Discarding is part of the same undo step.
- **Transparency:** the result keeps transparency, composited exactly as the layers stack. The one case where that would
  NOT look the same: a layer in a mixing blend (multiply, screen, overlay …) over a spot with nothing under it but the
  paper mixes with the paper on screen. Proposed (owner to confirm): detect it before flattening and offer "Freeze onto the
  paper" (the paper is baked in too: opaque, exactly what you see, like Photoshop's Flatten Image) beside "Keep
  transparency".
- **"Keep lines editable" checkbox** (owner, 2026-10-18, third message): Flatten collapses everything by default; ticking
  the box freezes all raster (pixels, slabs, realistic paint) but keeps every editable line. Built with §2's sheets, so
  nothing changes look: each layer's raster becomes one pixel sheet at its place in the order and the lines stay between
  them as lines. Flattening layers of lines only just gathers all the lines into one layer. Remembered with the hidden-layer
  choice. (Note for the sheet's words: memory is freed only for the raster; lines stay light.)
- **Animation (lead, 2026-10-18, owner asked):** Flatten never drops frames. Boards belong to the drawing, and two animation
  boards may not overlap (DocOps.validate), so layers that animate in the SAME board pair frame by frame (frames are the
  board's, so they line up); a layer that is still inside that board is copied into every frame as the sheet under the
  animated one (every frame looks as before); layers animating in DIFFERENT boards give one layer that animates in both.
  If overlapping animation boards are ever allowed (owner to decide), two timelines in one place can't share a layer:
  Flatten keeps them as separate layers and says so, with an explicit "bake to the frame you see" choice. Never silent.
- **Freeze layer** (layer menu): the same, for one layer in place (Photoshop's Rasterize Layer): its lines, slabs and
  realistic paint state become plain pixels; frees memory; one undo.
- Merge Down keeps §2 (the owner chose: the lower layer's lines stay editable).

## 4. Lock
A lock button on each layer row (and in its menu). A locked layer: no painting, erasing, fill, line edits, transforms,
merges into or out of it, or deletion without confirming; each refused in words (painting is already refused; Select
already skips locked layers, v1.2 H9). `Layer.locked` is already in the document; the canvas reads it at pen-down.

## 5. Apply mask
Bakes the mask into the layer's pixels (alpha × mask coverage) and removes the mask, ONE undo step. The layer's items flatten
first (the mask changes pixels under them), with the sentence. Media payload: tiles the mask hides fully are erased
(state zeroed, crush kept, as the media eraser does); partly hidden tiles settle to dry pixels.

## 6. Whole-drawing export
Export gains a scope chip row: **This board · Whole drawing**. Whole drawing offers **Flat picture** (PNG cropped to every
painted pixel at full resolution, through RegionRenderer with the paper) or **Layered file** (ORA via JB-2.14b; PSD when
JB-2.14c lands), plus "One picture per board" (a folder). The last scope and format are remembered; one tap swaps.
