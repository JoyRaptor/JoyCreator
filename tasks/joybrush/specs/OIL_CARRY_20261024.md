# Oil: stroke ends, covers, carry (oil specialist, 2026-10-23/24)

The owner's three oil jobs (2026-10-23), in his words and order:
1. **Stroke ends**: every oil brush but Palette knife 2 "starts and stops like a razor-cut perfect line".
2. **New colour over old paint is too hard**: "painting a different oil colour on top takes many dabs".
3. **The dirty brush**: a stroke through wet colours picks them up and carries them ("a little blue at its start and red
   at its end"), colours swirl, curl and scrape together, gloopy.

Reference photos: `tasks/joybrush/evidence/oil-refs-20261023/` (1 practice sheet, 2 blue/yellow impasto, 3 one green
stroke with a dry trailing end, 4 colours swirled and scraped).

Every behaviour below is **selectable beside the old one** (memory: keep-old-behaviour-selectable) until he picks.

## 1. Stroke ends (`PasteBrush.ends`, knob "Stroke ends": loaded brush / clean cut)

| part | uniform / function | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| hair lengths | `jb_pasteTip(a)`, in `jb_pasteCover` | each run of hair has its own length: the front (stroke end) and the tips (stroke start) are ragged per run, only ever inward (the footprint box holds) |
| landing | `u_land` (PasteStep.land: 1 at touchdown, 0 after 0.6 x width of travel) | a blob of paint piled toward the tips; a more ragged outline (`JB_LAND_RAG`); a light landing starts in streaks (weak hairs not touching yet) |
| firm landing | `jb_pasteFirmLand()` (land x pressure 0.4..0.9) | the hairs splay 14 % wider (`jb_pasteHalfW`, `PasteFootprint.LAND_SPLAY`), the round cap flattens square, a heavier ridge at the landing edge |
| lift-off | `u_lift` (PasteStep.lift: pressure under its recent peak, the peak relaxing over 12 mm) | each run's contact slides back toward its tip at its own pace, weak runs leave first, the paint thins (x0.4), its reach into dips falls off (`exp(-6 lift)`), it keys to the tooth faces rising toward the travel (`jb_pasteCatch`): broken streaks and speckle |
| no scraping while lifting | `jb_pastePlough()` | a lifting brush does not plough up the paint it just laid (that made Palette knife 1's weave grid and its ladder of step lines) |

Blades (Palette knife 2, Scraper) never see any of it. "clean cut" is pixel-identical to the engine before.

## 2. Covers (`u_cover`, lab `?pb=cover:x`; app: off until the owner picks)

Why it was hard: the brush's target height is measured from the CANVAS, so over wet paint as thick as its target it lays
nothing and ploughs the old paint up into its cells; one mixed paint per cell then lays that mixture (KM: 2-6 % ultramarine
in cadmium yellow is olive) for the rest of the stroke. Lab, yellow over wet blue, Oil round: 26 % yellow after one pass,
62 % after three; preset retunes (swap 0.95, mix 0.04) barely move the first pass (28 %).

Covers: pressed lightly (`1 - smoothstep(0.35, 1, p)`), on wet paint (`open`), loaded (`fill`), not already piled thick, a
brush rides on the wet layer and its target becomes up to a coat (`0.4 x thick x fill`) above what is there
(`jb_pasteRide`, used by BOTH passes so the brush loses what the canvas gains); a new coat's colour share is what an opaque
coat that thick hides, `1 - exp(-S d)`, instead of its volume share (`jb_pasteCoat`). Cover 1: Oil round 87 % in one pass.
It never loads from canvas sampling (guard: "no non-destructive canvas sampling for loading").

## 3. Carry (`u_carry`, lab `?pb=carry:x`; app: off until the owner picks)

The touching hairs (depth rows 0-5) hold `JB_HAIR_SHARE` 0.06 of a full cell, the belly near the ferrule (rows 6-7) most
of the load (`jb_pasteProf`, the loading tray fills to it: lab `pasteRowShare`, Kotlin twin owed in
`MediaLayerEngine.reloadBrush` before carry ships). Paint seeps between neighbouring depth cells by how full each is
(`jb_pasteSeep`). So colour picked up is laid back within a few centimetres and fades as the belly's paint comes through,
instead of tinting the whole load for the rest of the stroke. Target, reach and swap read a cell's fullness against its own
depth's share (`jb_cap`).

### Patent guard for carry (Lead, 2026-10-24: R8, US 8,462,173, the pickup-first rule E3+E4). Keep all three:
- **(a)** every cell holds ONE mixed paint (K, S, amount); never a separate picked-up amount beside a clean one.
- **(b)** deposit always comes from the contact cell's single mixed state; no rule compares a picked-up amount to a flow
  rate, and nothing deposits picked-up paint first.
- **(c)** the flow between depth cells is plain diffusion by fullness between NEIGHBOURS (`jb_pasteSeep`, both
  directions), never "refill from the reservoir when the pickup runs out". Dirty colour coming off first must EMERGE from
  mixing + flow, never be a branch.
