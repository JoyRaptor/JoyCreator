# Symmetry: many brushes at once (spec, 2026-10-19)

Status: SPEC ONLY, nothing built. Written by the JoyBrush Lead from the owner's request on 2026-10-19.

## 0. The owner's ask (condensed)

- It lives in the Helpers menu: the top-right icon with the triangle ruler.
- The guide is built like the existing Ruler: a very thin line across the whole screen with two points.
  - Drag one point to move just that end, or drag the line to move all of it.
  - Two fingers rotate it.
  - It snaps to vertical and horizontal.
- **It mirrors the brush, not the pixels.** It is like having two (or 30) brushes going at once. Watercolour on a vertical mirror must run DOWN on both sides. Impasto light and paper grain stay where they are on the page.
- A number slider you can scrub moves from a mirror to radial and back, up to 25–30 lines.
- Works with every brush: watercolour, oil, pencil, the digital pen / Unibrush, vector, everything.
- No hoops. Pick it, the line appears, and you paint.

## 1. What the person sees

1. Helpers (triangle-ruler icon) → **Symmetry**. A thin line appears through the middle of the screen, vertical, and painting works at once.
2. **The line** reuses the Ruler's widget:
   - two end handles: drag one to turn the line about the other;
   - drag the body to move the whole line;
   - two-finger rotate;
   - it runs edge to edge across the screen;
   - it snaps to 0° / 90° within about 3°, with a light haptic tick (45° snap: ask the owner).
3. **The number** is one scrubbable pill next to the line:
   - **1** = off;
   - **2** = mirror across the line;
   - **3–30** = radial copies around the centre;
   - a **Mirror** chip next to it turns radial into kaleidoscope (each radial copy also mirrored).
4. The guide is an overlay and never exports. It's saved with the drawing like the other guides (verify how Ruler persists; see E9).
5. Off: Helpers → Symmetry again, or scrub the number to 1.

## 2. The rule that makes it work: mirror the brush, not the pixels

Every copy is a full brush with its own transformed pen. The page's own physics stays in page space:
- watercolour gravity and tilt;
- paper tooth and grain;
- impasto lighting.

A mirrored wash therefore runs down on both sides, and its texture is the page's, not a flipped picture.

For copy *k*, the transform is a rotation by 2πk/N about the centre C, and for mirror copies a reflection across the line. It changes these parts of each pen sample:

| part of the pen sample | per copy |
|---|---|
| position | transformed |
| tilt direction (azimuth) | rotated; reflected for mirror copies. ⚠ The app uses y-down and the media lab y-up; see memory "tilt lean conventions differ" |
| stroke direction | follows from the transformed positions |
| pressure, speed, time | unchanged |
| fixed tip angle (calligraphy, Unibrush facing arrow) | rotated with the copy; mirror copies flip handedness |
| jitter / scatter seed | the SAME seed for every copy, so scatter is symmetric (recommended, see Q5) |

**E4 in detail (the helper's media review, 10-19):**
1. **Correctness, not just fit.** Water keeps running after the pen passes (settling, beads, drip tails, the quiet rule), but only inside the live patch. Outside it, water is frozen. With one patch, the copy under the pen keeps flowing while the other copies freeze, so a mirrored wash would NOT run down on both sides. A patch holding every copy (whole-screen patch, or the atlas) is required for the §2 promise.
2. **Patch moves.** The patch follows one pen. Copies spread over the page would force a move per copy every frame; a move is a full flush and reload (10–16 ms a frame on the Note 9's wet strokes). Wet radial paint would crawl from moves alone.
3. **Per-frame GPU cost, not only memory.** The water passes run over the bounding box of all wet paint. Scattered copies make that box screen-sized: about 4× today's 1024² every wet frame on the Note 9's 2960×1440 screen. Phase 3 likely needs "water only where water is" (the helper's parked item 1) back, or wet copies stay few.
4. **History.** Media strokes keep their own history (reportMediaHistory, the document-stamp guard). N copies must land as ONE undo step in MediaCanvas too, the same rule as E3.
5. **Wet copies mix.** Two wet copies meeting at the line share one water field, so their pigment adds up physically. For watercolour and oil the Q2 answer can't be "merge without extra darkening".
6. The atlas fits scattered copies best (memory by painted tiles, not screen size), but it's parked because tilted flux is 3.2–5.7× slower, and copies multiply that. It's an option only if the flux cost is solved.
7. "Pencil" in E4 means the media pencils (Proto Pencil … Woodless 8B). The stamp Pencil is plain GL (E3).
8. Tile board: tile cost today scales with touch events × tiles touched, and N copies multiply that. The helper's tile step 1 (one paint pass per frame on tile, 10-19) should land before symmetry is timed on tile.
9. Per-copy costs are measured per family in E10, not quoted.

**The single biggest cost is E4.** Without it, symmetry fully works for:
- stamp brushes, Unibrush, vector and the eraser;
- realistic paint only while every copy lands near the pen (a mirror line close to where you paint). Radial watercolour or oil would lose copies.

## 4. Phasing (by size; AI day estimates are unreliable, so none are given)

- **Phase 1 (phone-testable first slice):** E1 + E2 + E3 + E9, plus a minimal E8: Helpers → Symmetry shows a vertical line at screen centre, with the count scrubber. Ordinary brushes, Unibrush and vector copy; the eraser copies.
- **Phase 2:** E5 (grouped editable lines) and the full Ruler-style handles, two-finger rotate and snapping.
- **Phase 3:** realistic paint (E4): whole-screen patch (for correctness: every copy's water keeps moving), plus per-family caps, plus likely "water only where water is" for speed. Needs the owner's yes on the memory trade (see the 10-19 explanation: ~3× graphics memory, no help zoomed out).
- **Phase 4:** kaleidoscope chip, ghost cursors, 45° snap if wanted.

## 5. Questions for the owner

- **Q1** Radial centre: the line's midpoint (simplest), or a separate centre handle?
- **Q2** Where copies cross the line: merge as ONE stroke (no extra darkening; recommended) or overlap like two real brushes? This choice exists for ordinary brushes only. Wet watercolour and oil copies share one water field and always mix physically where they meet.
- **Q3** Watercolour/oil symmetry zoomed far out (where the whole-screen patch can't fit): refuse with a sentence ("zoom in to paint watercolour with symmetry"), or quietly use fewer copies?
- **Q4** Are per-family caps OK? A first guess, to be measured: ink/Unibrush 30, pencil about 16, watercolour about 8, oil about 4–8 on the Note 9.
- **Q5** Scatter and jitter mirrored exactly (symmetric; recommended), or each copy random?
- **Q6** Snap at 45° too, or only vertical/horizontal?

## 6. How others do it (from memory: NOT verified this session; a Haiku check before Phase 2 UI)

- **Infinite Painter:** Symmetry in its guides: mirror (vertical/horizontal), radial with a count, and kaleidoscope (radial + mirror). The guide moves and rotates on canvas. It works with all brushes because it duplicates the stroke, not the pixels.
- **Krita:** the Multibrush tool modes: Symmetry (radial N), Mirror (H/V), Translate, Snowflake (radial + mirror), Copy Translate. Separately, canvas Mirror tools (horizontal/vertical) with a movable, lockable axis handle.
