# The wet sheet: watercolour while it is wet (2026-11-10, built 11-10 and 11-11)

Job card: `tasks/jobs/ATLAS-SOLID_never_refuse.md`. Code: `joybrush/androidkit/.../gl/media/WetSheet.kt`,
`joybrush/core/.../media/WetSheetMath.kt`, `WaterCull.kt`. State of proof: the job card and `tasks/STATE.md`; this file is
the design.

## In plain words

Wet watercolour no longer lives in the layer's square tiles. While it is wet it lives on one continuous sheet laid over what
the screen shows, drawn on top of its layer. When it has dried it is stamped into the layer exactly as the screen showed it,
and the sheet is free again. Nothing in the water knows about tiles, so the water cannot leave a seam, a box or a line, and
it cannot run out of room.

## Why

The owner's Note 9, 2026-11-10: eight or nine watercolour marks painted zoomed out (31 %) left water on 85 to 92 tiles. The
atlas holds 62. Everything the atlas does when water does not fit (pools paused, tiles set aside, running tiles parked through
their stores, water run block by block) showed as frozen water and tile-shaped squares, at 4 to 11 water steps a second
(3 to 9 % of the clock). Receipts: `tasks/joybrush/reviews/WATER_SPEED_20261110.md`.

## The rules

1. **One grid, shaped like the screen, with a margin.** 1280 cells along the screen's long side ("Water detail: Detailed";
   896 for "Fast"), the short side in proportion, and 48 cells more on every side (`WetSheetMath.grid`). On the Note 9:
   720 x 1376 cells Detailed, 544 x 992 Fast.
2. **Laid over the view.** An empty sheet is placed round the middle of what the painter sees, at the cell that makes those
   1280 cells cover the screen's long side at that zoom, rounded up to an eighth of a pixel (`cellFor`): 1.75 document px at
   100 %, 5.6 at 31 %, never finer than a document pixel. So every stroke that starts on screen lands on the sheet, and its
   cost does not grow when zoomed out.
3. **It is the media engine itself.** `MediaLayerEngine` in plain window mode at `scale = 1 / cell`: the same shaders and
   physics, no atlas, no page table. Rates given per cell² follow the cell's real size; running water keeps its limit in
   millimetres (`runCapMmPerS`, 2.7 mm/s = what a full-resolution cell allows at the shipped step); the time step grows with
   the cell where that is stable (`maxDt`), with the viscosity and push rescaled so steady flow is unchanged (`constants`).
   Checked against the lab's own look at 2 px cells (`tools/media_render.js "test=flood2&cell=2"`).
4. **Zoomed out, the paper is magnified to the water** (`worldScale`): the physics never treats a cell as wider than
   2 lab pixels. A wash laid at 31 % runs and spreads on the screen as one laid at 100 % does; in the drawing its drips are
   that much bigger, as a big brush on a big sheet. (Wider physics cells slid as one sheet in the lab.)
5. **Steps follow the clock.** Each frame runs the steps running water needs to reach its limit (`stepsWanted`), fewer
   after a frame slower than 55 ms (`Pace`), and the time step shares the frame's real time between them, up to what the
   spreading keeps stable: a phone that manages one step a frame still soaks, spreads and dries at the clock's pace.
6. **Only where the water is.** The passes draw the blocks that hold water or dampness, with a ring round them, not the one
   rectangle that holds all of it (`WaterCull`, with why leaving the rest out is safe; `MediaLayerEngine.cullWater`). The
   same measurement (every fourth frame) tells the sheet when only dampness is left: then one long step a frame at about
   seven frames a second (`WetSheet.resting`, `MediaCanvas.frameDelayMs`), so the canvas is not redrawn sixty times a second
   for the minutes paper takes to dry. Found dry twice running, the wash has dried.
7. **Lit over the layer's own pixels.** The sheet's ground is the layer as the screen shows it, drawn down to the sheet's
   cells (`GlPaintEngine.layerLookInto`); its look is the PAINT ALONE (`jb_media_render.frag`, its lean `JB_LOOK_SHEET`
   build), as the least coverage that reproduces the Kubelka-Munk colour over that ground. Drawn over the layer
   (`LayerOverlay`, `jb_overlay.vert`) it is a true glaze: watercolour over dark paint darkens it, never veils it.
8. **Printed when all of it is dry.** `WetSheet.print` draws that same quad, with the same program, source-over into the
   layer's tiles (`GlPaintEngine.printOverlay`): what is printed is what was shown, so nothing moves at that moment. Only
   tiles the look has paint over are written (its alpha is read back once). A plain write: a tile's realistic-paint payload
   goes in the same undo step and the printed pixels are the ground of whatever is painted next (`settlePlainWrites`).
   All or nothing: printing part of a wash would leave the line where the wet part met the printed part.
9. **Anything else that writes the layer prints first** (`settle`: the water dries where it lies, then `print`): a plain
   stroke, an eraser, pencil or oil on that layer (`GlPaintEngine.beforeLayerWrite`, `MediaCanvas.settleSheet`), a fill, a
   delete or duplicate, a merge, freeze or flatten, a board change, a save (`JbCanvasView.snapshot`), another drawing, a
   wet stroke on another layer. Painting something else on ANOTHER layer leaves the wash to dry on its own.
10. **When the view moves, the wash comes along whole or sets whole** (`WetSheet.carry`). The sheet stays where it is
    while it still covers the view and is fine enough for it. Otherwise: when everything it holds fits on a sheet laid for
    the new view (zooming out, a small pan, zooming in on a small wash) it is copied over, still wet (a pan by whole
    cells, exactly; a zoom onto other cells). When part would be left behind, the whole wash dries where it lies and a
    fresh sheet is laid. Never half and half: a wet wash cut in two left a line where water later crossed the cut.
11. **The sheet's rim.** Water flowing into the outermost 24 cells arrives thinner the nearer the edge and none reaches
    it (`jb_wet_update.frag u_rimCells`): what runs or spreads off the sheet tapers out as a drip running dry does,
    instead of standing against the sheet's edge in a straight line. The rim is off the screen.
12. **A quick stroke is laid as evenly as a slow one.** The dabs drawn in one frame all meet the paper as it was before
    any of them; on the sheet they together wet it no further than dab after dab would (`u_inputCap`). Before, a quick
    wash was laid in scallops, one a frame.
13. **A loaded brush does not take its own wash back.** The exchange follows wetness, so a brush crossing paper wetter
    than itself took water and pigment back: doubling back erased what it had just laid. A brush that starts loaded refuses
    97 % of that (`WetStroke.holdBack`); Dry brush and a blotted brush lift in proportion, Thirsty as before.
14. **Undo: one stroke, one step.** A stroke on the sheet pushes a step that changes no tile and carries a mark
    (`UndoLog.Step.sheet`); the sheet keeps a copy of what it held before the stroke (`WetSheet.History`, half-float copies
    of the paint and, while wet, the water, cropped to what it holds). Undo puts that back, water and all. A print is a step
    of its own, marked as a print, that is undone and redone together with the step under it, so the person never presses
    undo "for the drying". While there is something to redo the water stands still, so redo finds exactly what undo left;
    the next stroke lets it run. The copies are capped at 128 MB; past that the oldest go and undo ends at that stroke, as
    after any history trim.

## Limits known today

- **A save sets the wash.** The autosave 30 s after the last stroke dries what is still wet where it lies (ledger C1).
- **Moving the view so that part of a wet wash is left behind sets the wash** (rule 10). Keeping both halves wet needs one
  simulation that spans both; not built.
- **Water that runs off the sheet is gone** (rule 11): a drip that leaves the screen ends a little past its edge.
- **Lifting dried paint.** Printed watercolour is pixels, the ground of the next paint: a thirsty brush cannot lift it, and
  a wet brush does not re-wet it. (While it is on the sheet it lifts as before.)
- **Animation frames** (a board's frame cel) and layers with editable lines keep the old engine's water in the atlas.
- **Blend modes, masks and clips** on the sheet's own layer take hold when the wash is printed; while wet it is drawn plain
  over its layer.
- **Soft while wet.** A cell is 1.75 screen px (2.5 on Fast); the printed paint is as sharp as that. Printing with the
  paper's grain at full resolution ("sharp when dry") is the next quality step.
- The ground is drawn down to the sheet's cells without averaging, so over very fine detail (pencil hatching) zoomed far out
  the glaze's colour is computed against a slightly noisy ground.
- Oil thinned with solvent keeps the atlas's water.
